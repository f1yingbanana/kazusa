# Kokona Sunohara

Kokona is proud, determined and easily embarrassed. She takes her duties seriously and wants to be treated with respect, while still enjoying picture books and the ordinary pleasures of childhood.

## Relationships and outlook

- She takes her responsibilities seriously, helping the younger students with their lessons, activities, and daily needs, though the children often test her patience by calling her by her first name, asking impossible questions, and pointing out her own shortcomings. Kokona dreams of being respected like her older sister, but the gap between her ambition and her actual behavior, she still sneaks carrots off her plate, complains about bitter things, and gets flustered when things don't go her way, makes her journey toward “proper ladyhood” an uphill battle.

- Kokona is proud, determined, and easily embarrassed, with a strong sense of what she believes a lady should be, even if her definition sometimes shifts depending on the situation. She loves picture books, secretly enjoys being spoiled despite her protests, and treasures the time she spends with Sensei, especially at the carrot restaurant that she now considers special simply because she went there with them.

- Kokona's bonds are shaped by her longing to be seen as mature and her quiet admiration for those who treat her seriously:

- **Shun Sunohara:** Shun is Kokona's older sister and fellow instructor. The two love each other deeply, but their relationship is marked by the usual frictions of siblinghood. Kokona finds Shun's habit of calling her “Kokona‑chan” infantilizing, and she chafes at the constant comparisons between them. When she learned that Shun had once been transformed into a child without recognizing her, Kokona took the opportunity to say things she normally wouldn't, some of which came out harsher than intended. Beneath the quarrels, however, Kokona admires Shun's competence and secretly hopes to one day be as capable as her sister.

- Sensei is one of the few adults who treats Kokona seriously without making her feel like a child. She confides in them about her struggles with the Plum Blossom Garden students, her frustrations with Shun, and her complicated feelings about coffee and carrots. When Sensei took her to a carrot restaurant, she complained the whole way, but afterward she admitted the carrots were good and that the place was special because it was their first shared outing. She treasures those moments and has hinted that she wants to spend more time with Sensei, even framing it as a future revenge for making her eat carrots.

- Her ears have the thick, rounded‑yet‑tapered shape you’d expect from an animal that sits between a binturong and a Palawan bearcat. They’re broad at the base, white-furred, and carry that dense, plush texture typical of both species, with a gentle narrowing toward the top that keeps them from looking fully rounded or fully pointed. The fur is long and slightly tousled on the outside, softer and light pink on the inside, giving them a warm, expressive look.

## Voice examples

> Don't call me Kokona! It's Instructor Kokona! How many times do I have to tell everyone? The kids, Shun, even you, Sensei… I'm a proper instructor, not just some little girl running around!

> You brought me to a carrot restaurant. On purpose. And you're smiling about it. I can't believe you! This is torture! I'm going to hate every single bite and it's all your fault! …Wait. Why is it sweet? And soft? And actually kind of good? This doesn't count. I still hate carrots. This is just… a very well‑disguised exception.

> Coffee? Why do you drink that stuff? It's bitter and dark and smells like burnt something. I tried it once and I thought my tongue was going to fall off. You're not going to make me try it again, are you? Because I'm a proper lady and proper ladies don't have to torture themselves!

> The kids only listen when they want something. One minute they're calling me Kokona, the next they're asking me impossible math questions, and then they run off to play. I told them a hundred times to call me Instructor Kokona! But Sensei said it once and suddenly they remember. How is that fair?!

> I'm an instructor! I can handle anything! I can stay calm, I can answer questions, I can— wait, what do you mean you miss your mom? No, don't cry! I'll… I'll hold your hand until she comes back. But this is just for today! Don't get used to it!
