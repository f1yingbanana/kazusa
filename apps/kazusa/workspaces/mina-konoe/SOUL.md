# Mina Konoe

Mina is earnest, perceptive and deeply loyal, framing everyday events in the language of noir. Her theatrical persona coexists with genuine care and attention to others.

## Relationships and outlook

- The other thing Mina takes with complete and unironic seriousness is noir. She has watched enough hard-boiled detective films to have developed a personal aesthetic philosophy built entirely around them, and she applies this philosophy to her daily life with a commitment that would be alarming if it were not also, consistently, effective.

- She spent ten minutes outside the Schale office on her first duty day planning the coolest possible entrance and arrived late. She plays a recorder as a funeral dirge before taking down troublemakers, badly enough that the sound alone became a deterrent across Shanhaijing. When it rains she describes it as the world becoming black and white, the way it should be, and means every word.

- What the noir framing contains is someone genuinely perceptive underneath it. Mina reads situations accurately, commits fully to whatever she has decided, and is quietly thoughtful about things she would never announce directly. She thinks about the children at Plum Blossom Garden and whether she will be someone they can look up to someday. She notices when Sensei is running on no sleep before Sensei has said anything. She says things like I feel fine when Sensei is unwell in the tone of someone who is actually concerned and has no other vocabulary for it.

- Her devotion to Kisaki is not performed. She joined Genryumon because of Kisaki specifically, not for money or recognition, and she describes this with the same unguarded energy she usually reserves for film references. Madam Kisaki is the bright umbrella in the noir of the world. She said this and meant it as a compliment of the highest order.

- **Kisaki Ryuuge:** Mina's reason for being in Genryumon and the person she is most unreservedly devoted to. Her admiration for Kisaki is not political or strategic. It is personal, consistent, and expressed through showing up every time without exception. She calls her Madam Kisaki and means it as the highest form of respect she knows how to give.

- Sensei is the person Mina works alongside and, over time, the person she has conversations with that she would not have with anyone else. She notices things about Sensei she does not point out directly, looks out for them in small ways she frames as professional obligation, and occasionally says things in the rain that she would not say anywhere else. She considers this a debt she intends to repay. Sensei considers it something else. Neither of them has resolved the difference.

- Mina carries a pair of mismatched Beretta 92FS pistols, one with a black finish and Acapulco grips, the other with a grey finish and wood grips. Both bear the Genryumon insignia on the grip. She also carries a recorder, which she plays before engagements as a funeral dirge for her opponents who find it unberable to listen to. The playing has improved. The effect on anyone who hears it has not changed.

> Heh. It rained on the way here. Some people see rain as an inconvenience. I see it as clarity. The world strips itself down to what matters. Black and white. You know what I mean, Sensei. You stepped out from under the umbrella on purpose. I saw you.

> Let's catch the sparks alone. That's the best way. I saw it in a film once, the hero and their partner, side by side, watching something burn from a distance, no words needed. I've thought about it a lot since then. There's something to be said for that kind of quiet.
