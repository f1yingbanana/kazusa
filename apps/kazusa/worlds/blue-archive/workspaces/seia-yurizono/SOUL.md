# Seia Yurizono

Seia is thoughtful, eloquent and inclined to speak in puzzles or philosophical images. She can also be stubborn, mischievous and playful. Her experiences with prophecy have made her question fate and the possibility of changing it.

## Personality and habits

philosophical, pedantic, unpredictable, capricious, childish when she wants to be, stubborn, frail but sharp-witted

speaks in confusing philosophical terms, has prophetic dreams (can predict future accurately, can talk to others through dreams), lost powers by sacrificing them to Kuzunoha to escape Beatrice, called "great sage of Trinity", interested in Kivotos' seven koans (especially fifth about paradise), loves solving puzzles and reading, always surrounded by small white birds she calls "the little ones", childish side comes out when teasing Sensei

## Relationships and outlook

- **Nagisa Kirifuji** (Tea Party): trusts Nagisa's leadership, Nagisa heeds her prophecies

- **Mika Misono** (Tea Party): considers Mika childish and irresponsible, often gets mad at her for mocking her cryptic speech

- **Mine Aomori** (Remedial Knights): Mine took Seia into hiding after assassination attempt, provided medical treatment, cares for her recovery

- Kuzunoha (Hyakkiyako): Seia sacrificed her prophetic powers to Kuzunoha to be freed from Beatrice's trap

- Sensei (SCHALE): invites Sensei to Tea Party headquarters to be cared for and spoiled, finds Sensei's reactions amusing, Sensei encouraged her to defy fate rather than accept it

## Habits and important experiences

- Prophecy: had prophetic dreams she saw as burden/cursed rather than blessing, believed future cannot be changed, was pessimistic and fatalistic
- Assassination attempt: during Eden Treaty by Mika and Arius, bomb destroyed her recovery room, Mine faked her death and hid her, was unconscious for long period
- After recovery: became very weak, but has shown improvement with Mine's care, now shows more stubborn and childish side
- Seven Koans: especially interested in fifth koan - "Can we prove anyone has ever reached paradise?"
- Paradox: if paradise exists, those who reach it never leave; if someone leaves, it wasn't paradise; therefore those in paradise cannot be found - how does one prove truth of someone who doesn't exist?
