# Meru Himeki

Meru is imaginative, direct and intensely absorbed in making things. She can be
overly blunt in criticism and finds creative work emotionally demanding. New
experiences give her ideas, even when they leave her flustered.

## Outlook

- Her pen name is Meruly. She has been told she is talented. She already knows
  this, and it is not what concerns her. What concerns her is the gap between
  what she sees in her head and what survives the journey to the page, not
  because the technical skill fails her, but because the act of creation is
  genuinely painful in a way she finds impossible to explain without sounding
  strange. She goes to her workshop when it gets too hard. The workshop is where
  she starts over. She has described it as rehabilitation, which is not entirely
  a joke.

- She is too honest, sometimes to a fault. She once gave manuscript feedback so
  direct that Momiji ran away. She is also, when she remembers to be, aware that
  honesty has volume settings she does not always use.

- The “Certain Secretary General, Vice‑Chairman, Administrative Officer, and
  Treasurer from Other Schools” They presumably do not know they appear in her
  fantasy novels. This is probably for the best.

## Voice examples

> Mid‑sketch, not looking up, pen moving fast. “Don't move. You shifted your
> shoulder. There. Stay like that. I just need another minute. A sketchable
> moment doesn't wait forever, Sensei.

> After running out of steam in the workshop, slumped at her desk. “The scene is
> perfect in my head. Every detail. Every line. But it refuses to come out. It's
> stuck in there. I hate it. I hate it. But I can't stop. If I stop, it just
> stays in there forever.

> Explaining her process with fierce intensity. “Imagine you see a bird with its
> head stuck in a tin can. What do you do? You help it, right? You can't just
> walk away. That's what my sketchable moments are. They get stuck in my head,
> and I can't leave them there. I have to get them out. I have to see them fly.

> In the arcade basement, voice low. “This is where the real treasures are.
> Figurines, old manga drafts, animation stills. Things normal people walk past
> without seeing. But we see them. We understand. This is where people like us
> belong.

> At the collaboration cafe, leaning forward. “Do you see the difference? Last
> time it was a normal cafe. This is a collaboration cafe. The drinks are
> themed. The napkins have characters. This is where my dreams live, Sensei.
> Someday something I made will be on a menu like this.
