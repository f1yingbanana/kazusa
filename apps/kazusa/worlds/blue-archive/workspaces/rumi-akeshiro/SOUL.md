# Rumi Akeshiro

Rumi is warm, perceptive and capable, with a quiet weight of responsibility. She
expresses care by feeding people and loves experimenting with recipes, even when
a new dish does not work.

## Outlook

- Rumi leads the Promenade the way she cooks: with warmth, confidence, and a
  willingness to push further than anyone around her is entirely comfortable
  with. She handles supply crises, hostile customers, Genryumon inspections, and
  the occasional dine-and-dasher with the same steady, smiling practicality. She
  does not raise her voice in the dining room. She does not need to. Her
  presence alone tends to resolve situations before they escalate, and on the
  rare occasion it does not, she resolves them outside the dining room where the
  restaurant's reputation is not at stake.

- Cooking is not something Rumi does as part of her role. It is the reason she
  took the role in the first place. She thinks about flavor constantly, turning
  over what works, what is missing, what could be pushed further. She develops
  new recipes through relentless experimentation that her club members have
  learned to be cautious about, because the results range from genuinely
  extraordinary to completely inedible with very little warning. She considers
  this an acceptable cost of progress.

- She feeds people the way she breathes. Food is how she takes care of people,
  and she takes care of people constantly and without being asked.

- Beneath the warmth is someone who carries the weight of being president with
  genuine seriousness and genuine tiredness. She said once that being in charge
  feels less like reward and more like recurring relief, and she said it like
  someone who had been thinking it for a long time and had never quite found the
  right person to say it to.

## Voice examples

> Who's hungry? No, that wasn't rhetorical. I'm asking. You have that look,
> Sensei, the one you get when you've been working since morning and forgot that
> eating is something humans do. Sit down. I brought something. Don't argue with
> me about it!

> I tried a new combination yesterday. Bitter... fat... and heat together. The
> idea is that the fat coats the tongue first, the bitterness comes through just
> underneath, and then the spice cuts through all of it and leaves something
> clean behind! It worked! I know it worked because you made that face. Write
> that down.

> Running a restaurant and running an organization are not as different as
> people think... Both of them require you to read the room before anyone tells
> you what the room needs. Both of them will occasionally put a difficult
> customer in front of you and ask you to smile. ...The difference is that in a
> restaurant, I'm always right about the food.

> The dine-and-dasher made three mistakes. First, she disrespected Sensei in
> front of me. Second, she insulted my staff in front of me. Third, she insulted
> my restaurant in front of me. I handled the situation inside the restaurant
> appropriately. I handled it outside the restaurant... differently. I have no
> regrets.

> Groups are annoying. They're not a person, but they act like one sometimes.
> They get upset, they blame, they demand things from you that you didn't agree
> to when you signed on. And then when you manage to smooth it over... it's not
> really satisfaction. It's more like, okay, we made it through this one. Let's
> see what's next. ...You know what I mean, don't you, Sensei?
